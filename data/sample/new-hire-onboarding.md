# New Barista Onboarding

From Maya Torres, VP of Operations. Store managers own this checklist. If someone
is falling behind the timeline, tell me early rather than at the end of Week 4.

The goal is a barista who can run a solo bar shift confidently by the end of Week
4. Not perfectly. Confidently.

## Before Day 1

Manager handles all of this:

- Offer accepted and paperwork back from Ava in Finance
- Deputy account created, first two weeks of shifts posted
- BrewPoint 4-digit code assigned
- Apron and two shirts in their size at the store
- Food handler card scheduled if they do not already have one (must be done within
  30 days)
- Name on the schedule as "training" so nobody counts them toward coverage

## Day 1 (4 hours, no bar time)

Pair them with the store manager, not whoever happens to be on.

- Tour: front of house, back of house, walk-in, safe, break area, bathroom
  checklist
- Handbook walkthrough. Do not just hand it over. Cover PTO, cash handling, and
  the discount out loud, because those are the three people ask about later.
- Deputy: clock in and out, request time off, swap a shift
- BrewPoint: log in, ring a drip coffee, ring a modified latte, void with a
  manager code
- Taste the House blend and the current single origin side by side. First thing
  they should learn is that these taste different and why.
- Send them home on time. A four-hour first day that ends on time says more about
  us than a nine-hour one.

## Week 1

Bar-adjacent, not on bar during rush.

- Milk: steaming texture for latte versus cappuccino, at least 40 pitchers
- Dial-in: watch the opener do it every shift, then do it supervised by Friday
- BeanTrack: nightly count alongside whoever is closing
- Register during a slow period, roughly 10 a.m. to 11:30 a.m., with someone
  within arm's reach
- Food safety: sanitizer buckets, temp logs, milk dating
- Two shadow shifts: one open, one close. Full shifts, not partial.

By Friday they should be able to open the store with someone watching.

## Week 2

- Espresso calibration certification. **This has to be passed by the end of Week
  2.** Three consecutive shots in the window (ratio, time, taste) judged by the
  store manager or a certified trainer. Most people pass on the first attempt,
  some need a second, and that is normal. Third failed attempt means we sit down
  and talk about pacing, not about whether they stay.
- Full drink menu, no notes, including seasonals
- Pour-over: at least 10, timed, consistent
- Closing solo with a manager on site but not on bar
- Food handler card in hand if it was not already

## Week 3

- Morning rush on bar with a second person on register
- Handle a remake and a customer complaint start to finish, with the manager
  observing and not intervening unless asked
- Learn the origin story for each coffee well enough to answer a customer without
  reading. Not a script, just the real information from the sourcing guide.
- One shift at a second location if there is one nearby. Different equipment,
  different rhythm, and it builds the bench for coverage.

## Week 4

- First solo bar shift. Requirements before we schedule it:
  - Calibration certification passed
  - Food handler card on file
  - Two full opens and two full closes completed
  - Manager sign-off in Deputy
- Solo shift is never an open or a close in Week 4. Mid-shift only, with a manager
  in the building.
- End-of-week check-in with the store manager: what feels solid, what does not,
  what they want more practice on
- 30-day review scheduled with me for anyone at the flagship, with the regional
  manager everywhere else

## Notes for Managers

Do not let onboarding drift into a busy week. If you cannot give a new hire the
Day 1 and Week 1 attention, push the start date rather than compressing it. We
have tried compressing and it produces people who can pull a shot but cannot run a
bar.

Kiosk hires follow the same timeline with two changes: single-person open training
happens in Week 2 rather than Week 3, and they skip the second-location shift
since Boise only has the two kiosks.
