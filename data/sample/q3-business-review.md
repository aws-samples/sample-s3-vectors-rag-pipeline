# Q3 2026 Business Review

**To:** Leadership team
**From:** Wally Brennan
**Date:** October 14, 2026

Good quarter. Not a clean one, but a good one. Numbers below, then the parts that
are keeping me up.

## Revenue

Q3 came in at **$4.2M, up 11 percent** over Q3 last year. Twelve straight quarters
of growth now, which I do not take for granted.

By region:

| Region | Locations | Q3 Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portland metro | 8 | $1.82M | +7% |
| Seattle | 5 | $1.24M | +14% |
| Eugene | 2 | $410K | +9% |
| Bend | 1 | $265K | +22% |
| Boise (kiosks) | 2 | $198K | +31% |
| Wholesale | n/a | $186K | +19% |
| Online | n/a | $92K | +34% |

Bend and Boise are small enough that the percentages flatter them, but both are
outperforming the model we underwrote them on. The Boise kiosks in particular
are worth studying. Low buildout cost, tight menu, and they are profitable in
month seven.

## Online and Subscription

Online grew **34 percent** and subscriptions are the whole story. We ended Q3 with
1,340 active subscribers against 1,010 at the end of Q2. Churn is 6.2 percent
monthly, which Ava tells me is respectable for coffee but I would like under 5.

The Ethiopian Yirgacheffe drove most of the new signups when it came back in
August. There is a lesson there about launching subscription pushes against a
seasonal arrival rather than a calendar date.

## Wholesale

Priya added **12 new accounts**: 7 restaurants, 3 hotels, and 2 office clients.
The Nines hotel account alone is running about $4K a month. Wholesale is 4.4
percent of revenue and I think it should be closer to 10 within two years. It is
the least capital-intensive growth we have.

Private label is steady. Both grocery partners renewed, no change in terms.

## What Is Not Working

**Green coffee costs are up 18 percent.** The Brazil frost in July moved the whole
market, not just Brazilian coffee. We have not raised menu prices and I do not
want to before January. That is compressing margin by roughly 3 points and it is
the single biggest issue on this list. Derek and Priya are looking at whether we
lock in 2027 Colombia volume early.

**Seattle staffing is tight.** We are running 4 open barista roles across 5 stores
and have been for most of the quarter. Two stores have cut weekday hours by 90
minutes. Maya is piloting a $1.50/hour differential for Seattle and we will know
by December whether it moves anything.

**Downtown Portland foot traffic is still soft.** The flagship is at about 84
percent of 2024 weekday volume. Weekends are fine, actually above. It is the
Monday-through-Thursday lunch crowd that has not come back, and I do not think it
is coming back on its own. We need to give people a reason that is not commuting.

## Q4 Initiatives

**Cold brew RTD line.** Launching November with the Honey Cardamom and a straight
black. Canned, 12 oz, targeting our own stores plus the wholesale accounts first
before we approach grocery. Packaging contract is signed. This is the biggest new
thing we are doing and it has the most ways to go wrong.

**Mobile ordering pilot.** Three locations: Portland flagship, Seattle Capitol
Hill, Bend. BrewPoint has the module and Sam is scoping the rollout. If the
flagship can capture even part of that lunch crowd through ahead-ordering, it
changes the downtown math.

**Second roasting facility, Boise.** Exploratory only. The Portland roaster is at
roughly 78 percent capacity and if wholesale grows the way I want, we hit the
ceiling in 2028. Boise is cheaper and closer to the inland accounts. Ava is
building the model, and I am not committing to anything this year.

Talk through all of it Thursday.
